This Executive Order is issued pursuant to Sections 1 and 2 of Chapter III of the Standing Orders of the Board of Trustees of The California State University.

Authority to Adopt and Revise Campus Policies Effective August 4, 1961, the Board of Trustees approved a "Statement of General Principles in the Delegation of Authority and Responsibility" which delegated to the campuses of the then California State Colleges, among other things, the authority to develop and revise from time to time policies relating to intellectual property rights and responsibilities together with protocols associated with those rights and responsibilities. Campuses have since that time developed and revised policies that address both copyrights and patents together with appropriate protocols.
This Executive Order is issued to gather into one document and to highlight the importance of Intellectual property rights to the California State University system. A number of develop ments have combined to encourage this emphasis: the maturing of the California State University as a modem, comprehensive university, changes in information technology, and new emphases in technology transfer as part of the University's mission. In light of this change in environment, presidents should ensure that campus policies addressing intellectual property are current and comprehensive. After review of the intellectual property policies on the campus, presidents are encouraged to submit them for review by the Office of General Counsel.

Authority to License or Sell Intellectual Property

Authority to transfer intangible intellectual property rights for valuable consideration has also been delegated to the campus presidents for some years. The most recent expression of this delegation appears in Executive Orders 370 (Issued December 31, 1981) and 409 (Issued January 5, 1983). These Executive Orders, among other things, conveyed to campus presidents the authority sell personal property belonging to the California State University when the president determines that the sale is in the best interests of the California State University and the sale is for consideration based on fair market value.
Contracts which are proposed to be executed for the transfer of these rights should also be reviewed by the Office of General Counsel prior to execution.
