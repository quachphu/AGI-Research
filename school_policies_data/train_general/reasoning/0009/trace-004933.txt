To solve this problem, we need to determine the winner of a chess game variant where two queens (white and black) are positioned on an n x n board. The game is played optimally by both players, with white moving first. The solution hinges on the parity of the board size, n.

### Approach
1. **Problem Analysis**:
   - The white queen starts at (1, 1) and the black queen at (1, n).
   - Queens can capture enemy pieces (including the opponent's queen) if there's a clear path.
   - The player loses if they cannot capture a piece on their turn or if their queen was captured in the previous move.

2. **Key Insight**:
   - For even n: The number of pawns between the two queens in the same row is even. White can capture these pawns in sequence and eventually capture the black queen.
   - For odd n: The number of pawns between the queens is odd. After capturing all pawns, it becomes black's turn to capture the white queen.

3. **Algorithm**:
   - Check if n is even or odd.
   - If n is even, white wins by capturing pawns in sequence starting with (1, 2).
   - If n is odd, black wins as they will capture the white queen after optimal moves.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
if n % 2 == 0:
    print("white")
    print("1 2")
else:
    print("black")
```

### Explanation
- **Even n**: The white queen can capture the pawns in the same row (1, 2), (1, 3), etc., leading to a clear path to the black queen. This results in white winning.
- **Odd n**: After capturing all pawns, it becomes black's turn to capture the white queen, leading to black's victory.

This approach efficiently determines the winner based on the parity of n, ensuring optimal play and correct moves for both players.
