To solve this problem, we need to efficiently handle two types of queries on a permutation of beavers: determining the minimum number of sessions required to shave a range of beavers and swapping two beavers in the permutation. The key insight is that the number of sessions required is determined by the number of "breaks" in the consecutive sequence of beavers in the permutation.

### Approach
1. **Understanding Sessions**: A session can shave a range of beavers if their positions in the permutation form a consecutive increasing sequence. For example, the permutation [1, 3, 4, 2, 5] requires 2 sessions to shave 1-5 because the sequence 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 is not contiguous in the permutation.

2. **Breaks in Sequence**: The number of breaks (i.e., where the position of a beaver is followed by a smaller number) determines the number of sessions. For a range [x, y], the number of sessions is 1 plus the number of breaks in the range.

3. **Efficient Updates with Fenwick Tree**: To handle dynamic updates (swaps) and range queries efficiently, we use a Fenwick Tree (Binary Indexed Tree) to maintain the count of breaks. This allows us to quickly update the structure when elements are swapped and to query the number of breaks in a range efficiently.
