To solve this problem, we need to determine how many integers between two nonnegative integers \( a \) and \( b \) (inclusive) are divisible by a given positive integer \( x \). Given the constraints where \( a \) and \( b \) can be as large as \( 10^{18} \), a brute force approach is infeasible. Instead, we use a mathematical approach to efficiently compute the result.

### Approach
The key insight is to use the properties of integer division to count the multiples of \( x \) within the range \([a, b]\):
1. The number of multiples of \( x \) up to \( b \) is given by \( \left\lfloor \frac{b}{x} \right\rfloor \).
2. The number of multiples of \( x \) up to \( a-1 \) is given by \( \left\lfloor \frac{a-1}{x} \right\rfloor \).
3. The difference between these two counts gives the number of multiples of \( x \) in the range \([a, b]\).

### Solution Code
```python
a, b, x = map(int, input().split())
count = (b // x) - ((a - 1) // x)
print(count)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input:** The input consists of three integers \( a \), \( b \), and \( x \) which are read and parsed.
2. **Compute Multiples up to \( b \):** Using integer division \( b // x \), we get the count of multiples of \( x \) from 1 to \( b \).
3. **Compute Multiples up to \( a-1 \):** Similarly, \( (a - 1) // x \) gives the count of multiples of \( x \) from 1 to \( a-1 \).
4. **Calculate Result:** The difference between the two counts gives the number of multiples of \( x \) in the inclusive range \([a, b]\).

This approach efficiently computes the result in constant time \( O(1) \), making it suitable for very large values of \( a \), \( b \), and \( x \).
