To solve this problem, we need to minimize the cost of reducing a number \( n \) to 1 using two operations: subtracting 1 (costs \( A \) coins) and dividing by \( k \) (costs \( B \) coins, only allowed if the number is divisible by \( k \)). The strategy involves choosing the most cost-effective operation at each step.

### Approach
1. **Special Case for \( k = 1 \)**: If \( k \) is 1, division is not feasible as dividing by 1 does not change the number. Therefore, the only option is to subtract 1 repeatedly, resulting in a cost of \( (n-1) \times A \).

2. **Iterative Reduction**: For \( k > 1 \), iteratively reduce the number \( x \) starting from \( n \) until it becomes 1:
   - **Subtract to Make Divisible**: If \( x \) is not divisible by \( k \), subtract the remainder to make it divisible by \( k \).
   - **Compare Costs**: If \( x \) is divisible by \( k \), compare the cost of subtracting down to \( x/k \) versus dividing. Choose the cheaper option.
   - **Handle Small Values**: If \( x \) becomes smaller than \( k \), subtract the remaining steps to reach 1.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
k = int(input())
A = int(input())
B = int(input())

if k == 1:
    print((n - 1) * A)
else:
    total = 0
    x = n
    while x > 1:
        if x < k:
            total += (x - 1) * A
            break
        remainder = x % k
        if remainder == 0:
            subtract_cost = (x - x // k) * A
            if B < subtract_cost:
                total += B
                x = x // k
            else:
                total += subtract_cost
                x = x // k
        else:
            total += remainder * A
            x -= remainder
    print(total)
```

### Explanation
- **Special Case Handling**: Directly handle the case where \( k = 1 \) since division by 1 is not useful.
- **Iterative Process**: Use a loop to reduce \( x \) step-by-step:
  - **Subtract to Make Divisible**: When \( x \) is not divisible by \( k \), subtract the remainder to make it divisible.
  - **Cost Comparison**: For \( x \) divisible by \( k \), compare the cost of subtracting down to \( x/k \) versus the cost of a single division. Choose the cheaper option.
  - **Final Steps**: If \( x \) becomes smaller than \( k \), directly subtract the remaining steps to reach 1.

This approach ensures that we always choose the most cost-effective operation, leading to the minimum total cost. The solution efficiently handles large values by reducing \( x \) iteratively, ensuring logarithmic complexity relative to \( n \) when divisions are feasible.
