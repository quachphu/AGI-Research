To solve this problem, we need to generate all integers between A and B (inclusive) that are either within the K smallest integers or the K largest integers in that range. The solution should efficiently handle large ranges up to 10^9 while ensuring the output is sorted in ascending order.

### Approach
1. **Identify Ranges**: Determine two ranges within the given interval [A, B]:
   - The first K smallest integers starting from A.
   - The last K largest integers ending at B.
   
2. **Handle Overflows**: Ensure that the ranges do not exceed the bounds of A and B. For example, if the total number of integers between A and B is less than K, the entire range is considered for both the smallest and largest integers.

3. **Combine and Deduplicate**: Merge the two ranges, remove duplicates, and sort the result to produce the final output.

### Solution Code
```python
A, B, K = map(int, input().split())

front_start = A
front_end = min(A + K - 1, B)
front_numbers = list(range(front_start, front_end + 1))

back_start = max(B - K + 1, A)
back_end = B
back_numbers = list(range(back_start, back_end + 1))

combined = set(front_numbers + back_numbers)
for num in sorted(combined):
    print(num)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input values A, B, and K are read from standard input.
2. **Front Range Calculation**: The front range starts at A and spans up to K elements, ensuring it does not exceed B.
3. **Back Range Calculation**: The back range ends at B and spans up to K elements backwards, ensuring it does not go below A.
4. **Combining Ranges**: Both ranges are combined into a set to remove duplicates, then sorted and printed in ascending order.

This approach efficiently handles large ranges by focusing on the necessary segments without generating the entire list of integers between A and B, thus optimizing both time and space complexity.
