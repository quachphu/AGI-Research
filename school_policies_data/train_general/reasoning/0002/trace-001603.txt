To solve this problem, we need to find the smallest positive integer \( x \) that is divisible by a given integer \( n \) and ends with at least \( k \) zeros. This is known as the \( k \)-rounding of \( n \).

### Approach
The key insight is that a number ending with at least \( k \) zeros must be divisible by \( 10^k \). Therefore, the solution involves finding the least common multiple (LCM) of \( n \) and \( 10^k \). The LCM of two numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of both. The formula for LCM using the greatest common divisor (GCD) is \( \text{LCM}(a, b) = \frac{a \times b}{\text{GCD}(a, b)} \).

Steps:
1. Compute \( 10^k \).
2. Calculate the GCD of \( n \) and \( 10^k \).
3. Use the GCD to compute the LCM of \( n \) and \( 10^k \).

### Solution Code
```python
import math

n, k = map(int, input().split())
ten_power = 10 ** k
gcd_val = math.gcd(n, ten_power)
result = (n * ten_power) // gcd_val
print(result)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of two integers \( n \) and \( k \).
2. **Compute \( 10^k \)**: This gives the number that must divide the result to ensure \( k \) trailing zeros.
3. **Calculate GCD**: The GCD of \( n \) and \( 10^k \) helps in determining the LCM efficiently.
4. **Compute LCM**: Using the formula \( \text{LCM}(a, b) = \frac{a \times b}{\text{GCD}(a, b)} \), we find the smallest number that is a multiple of both \( n \) and \( 10^k \), ensuring it ends with at least \( k \) zeros and is divisible by \( n \).

This approach efficiently combines mathematical properties of divisors and multiples to solve the problem in optimal time complexity, leveraging Python's built-in GCD function for accuracy and performance.
