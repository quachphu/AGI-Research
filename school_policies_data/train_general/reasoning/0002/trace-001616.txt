To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of cookies remaining with Takahashi and Aoki after Takahashi performs a series of actions K times. The actions involve eating his own cookies first and then Aoki's cookies if he runs out of his own.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Actions**: Takahashi will eat his own cookies first until they are exhausted. If he still needs to perform more actions (i.e., K is larger than the number of his cookies), he will then start eating Aoki's cookies.
2. **Mathematical Calculation**: Instead of simulating each action step-by-step (which is inefficient for large values of K), we can compute the remaining cookies using mathematical operations:
   - Calculate how many cookies Takahashi will have left after eating his own cookies.
   - Calculate the remaining actions after Takahashi's cookies are exhausted.
   - Use the remaining actions to determine how many cookies Aoki will have left.

### Solution Code
```python
a, b, k = map(int, input().split())
takahashi = max(a - k, 0)
remaining_k = max(k - a, 0)
aoki = max(b - remaining_k, 0)
print(takahashi, aoki)
```

### Explanation
1. **Takahashi's Cookies**: The number of cookies Takahashi will have left is determined by subtracting K from his initial count, but not less than zero. This is done using `max(a - k, 0)`.
2. **Remaining Actions**: After Takahashi's cookies are exhausted, any remaining actions (if K was larger than his initial cookie count) are calculated using `max(k - a, 0)`.
3. **Aoki's Cookies**: The remaining actions are then applied to Aoki's cookies, similarly ensuring his count does not drop below zero using `max(b - remaining_k, 0)`.

This approach efficiently handles the large input values by avoiding iterative steps and directly computing the results using simple arithmetic operations.
