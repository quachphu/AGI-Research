### Approach
1. **Maximize First-Degree Diplomas**: Start by assigning the maximum possible number of first-degree diplomas. This is determined by the minimum of the maximum allowed first-degree diplomas and the remaining participants after accounting for the minimum required second and third-degree diplomas.
2. **Calculate Remaining Participants**: Subtract the number of first-degree diplomas from the total number of participants to get the remaining participants who will receive second and third-degree diplomas.
3. **Maximize Second-Degree Diplomas**: Within the remaining participants, maximize the number of second-degree diplomas. This is constrained by the minimum of the maximum allowed second-degree diplomas and the remaining participants after accounting for the minimum required third-degree diplomas.
4. **Assign Third-Degree Diplomas**: The remaining participants after assigning first and second-degree diplomas will receive third-degree diplomas. This is guaranteed to be within the valid range due to the problem constraints.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
min1, max1 = map(int, input().split())
min2, max2 = map(int, input().split())
min3, max3 = map(int, input().split())

# Determine the maximum possible first-degree diplomas
first = min(max1, n - min2 - min3)

a = max(min2, remaining - max3)
b = min(max2, remaining - min3)
second = b

# The third-degree diplomas are the remainder
third = remaining - second

print(first, second, third)
```

3. **Remaining Participants**: After assigning the first-degree diplomas, the remaining participants are calculated.
4. **Second-Degree Calculation**: The maximum possible second-degree diplomas are determined by ensuring that the remaining participants after assigning second-degree diplomas can still meet the minimum and maximum constraints for third-degree diplomas.
