To solve this problem, we need to determine the maximum possible product of integers \( x \) and \( y \) where \( x \) is within the range \([a, b]\) and \( y \) is within the range \([c, d]\). The solution must efficiently compute this maximum product without iterating through all possible values in the ranges, given the constraints.

### Approach
The key insight is that the maximum product of two numbers from their respective ranges can be found by evaluating the products of the endpoints of these ranges. This is because the maximum product will always be achieved at one of the four corners formed by the endpoints of the ranges. Specifically, the possible candidates for the maximum product are:
- \( a \times c \)
- \( a \times d \)
- \( b \times c \)
- \( b \times d \)

By evaluating these four products and selecting the maximum value, we can efficiently determine the solution.

### Solution Code
```python
a, b, c, d = map(int, input().split())
max_product = max(a*c, a*d, b*c, b*d)
print(max_product)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of four integers \( a, b, c, \) and \( d \), which define the ranges for \( x \) and \( y \).
2. **Compute Products**: Calculate the four possible products using the endpoints of the ranges: \( a \times c \), \( a \times d \), \( b \times c \), and \( b \times d \).
3. **Determine Maximum**: Find the maximum value among these four products using the `max` function.
4. **Output Result**: Print the maximum product.

This approach ensures that we efficiently compute the result in constant time \( O(1) \), making it suitable even for the upper constraint values.
