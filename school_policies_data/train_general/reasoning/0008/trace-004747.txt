To solve this problem, we need to determine how many numbers between two given integers \( l \) and \( r \) (both inclusive) are divisible by another integer \( k \). The solution should efficiently compute this count without iterating through all numbers in the range.

### Approach
The key insight here is to use mathematical properties to compute the count of numbers divisible by \( k \) within the range \([l, r]\). 

1. **Understanding Divisibility**: A number \( x \) is divisible by \( k \) if \( x \% k == 0 \).
2. **Count Using Integer Division**: The number of integers up to \( r \) that are divisible by \( k \) can be calculated using integer division as \( r // k \). Similarly, the number of integers up to \( l-1 \) that are divisible by \( k \) is \( (l-1) // k \). The difference between these two values gives the count of numbers between \( l \) and \( r \) that are divisible by \( k \).

### Solution Code
```python
def count_divisible():
    l, r, k = map(int, input().split())
    count = (r // k) - ((l - 1) // k)
    print(count)

count_divisible()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of three space-separated integers \( l \), \( r \), and \( k \). These are read and parsed into integer variables.
2. **Mathematical Calculation**: 
   - The count of numbers up to \( r \) divisible by \( k \) is calculated as \( r // k \).
   - The count of numbers up to \( l-1 \) (to exclude numbers before \( l \)) divisible by \( k \) is calculated as \( (l-1) // k \).
   - The difference between these two counts gives the total numbers between \( l \) and \( r \) that are divisible by \( k \).

This approach efficiently computes the result in constant time \( O(1) \), making it very efficient even for the upper limits of the input constraints.
