To solve this problem, we need to determine the sum of the lengths of all 12 edges of a rectangular parallelepiped given the areas of its three adjacent faces. The solution involves calculating the individual edge lengths from the given areas and then summing them up.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Problem**: The given areas correspond to three adjacent faces of the parallelepiped. If we denote the edge lengths as \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), the areas can be represented as \(ab\), \(ac\), and \(bc\) in some order.
2. **Product of Areas**: The product of the three given areas \((ab \times ac \times bc)\) is equal to \((abc)^2\). Taking the square root of this product gives the product of the edge lengths \(abc\).
3. **Calculating Edge Lengths**: Each edge length can be derived by dividing the product of two areas by the third area and taking the square root of the result. This works because the given areas are permutations of \(ab\), \(ac\), and \(bc\).

### Solution Code
```python
s = list(map(int, input().split()))
s1, s2, s3 = s

a_sq = (s1 * s2) // s3
a = int(a_sq ** 0.5)

b_sq = (s1 * s3) // s2
b = int(b_sq ** 0.5)

c_sq = (s2 * s3) // s1
c = int(c_sq ** 0.5)

print(4 * (a + b + c))
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of three integers representing the areas of the three adjacent faces.
2. **Edge Length Calculation**:
   - For each edge length \(a\), compute \((s1 \times s2) // s3\) and take the square root.
   - Similarly, compute \(b\) and \(c\) using the respective areas.
3. **Summing Edges**: Each edge length appears four times in the parallelepiped. Summing the lengths of \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) and multiplying by 4 gives the total sum of all edges.

This approach efficiently computes the required edge lengths using algebraic manipulation and ensures correctness by leveraging the properties of the given areas.
