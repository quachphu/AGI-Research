To solve this problem, we need to find two rational numbers \( x/y \) and \( u/v \) such that \( u/v < \sqrt{p} < x/y \) and there are no other elements of the set \( Q_n \) between them. The set \( Q_n \) consists of all reduced fractions \( a/b \) where \( a \) and \( b \) are positive integers less than or equal to \( n \).

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Problem**: Given a prime number \( p \) and an integer \( n \) (where \( n > \sqrt{p} \)), we need to find the closest rational numbers from \( Q_n \) that bracket \( \sqrt{p} \). The upper bound \( x/y \) should be the smallest fraction greater than \( \sqrt{p} \), and the lower bound \( u/v \) should be the largest fraction less than \( \sqrt{p} \).

2. **Binary Search for Precision**: For each possible denominator \( y \) from 1 to \( n \), compute the smallest numerator \( x \) such that \( x/y > \sqrt{p} \) (upper bound) and the largest numerator \( x \) such that \( x/y < \sqrt{p} \) (lower bound). This is done using binary search to avoid floating-point precision issues.

3. **Efficient Comparison**: Use cross-multiplication to compare fractions without converting them to floating points, ensuring accuracy.

4. **Reduction Check**: Ensure that each fraction is in its reduced form by checking the greatest common divisor (gcd) of the numerator and denominator.

import math
import sys

def main():

                    if x_upper * best_upper_den < best_upper_num * y:

            low, high = 1, n

                    if x_lower * best_lower_den > best_lower_num * y:
