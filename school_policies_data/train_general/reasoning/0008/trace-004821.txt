To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of ways Tanechka can choose two distinct toys such that their total cost is exactly k burles. The toys are numbered from 1 to n, and each toy can only be chosen once. The pairs (a, b) and (b, a) are considered the same, and pairs where a equals b are not allowed.

### Approach
1. **Constraints Analysis**:
   - The smallest possible sum of two distinct toys is 3 (1 + 2).
   - The largest possible sum of two distinct toys is 2n - 1 (n + (n-1)).

2. **Key Observations**:
   - For two toys a and b (a < b) to sum to k, a must be in the range [1, floor((k-1)/2)].
   - Additionally, b must be within the range [1, n], which implies a must be at least (k - n).

3. **Range Calculation**:
   - **Lower Bound**: The minimum value of a is max(1, k - n) to ensure b (k - a) does not exceed n.
   - **Upper Bound**: The maximum value of a is min((k-1) // 2, n-1) to ensure a < b and both a and b are within the valid range [1, n].

4. **Count Valid Pairs**:
   - If the calculated upper bound is greater than or equal to the lower bound, the number of valid pairs is the difference between the upper and lower bounds plus one. Otherwise, there are no valid pairs.

### Solution Code
```python
n, k = map(int, input().split())
lower = max(1, k - n)
upper = min((k - 1) // 2, n - 1)
print(max(0, upper - lower + 1) if upper >= lower else 0)
```

### Explanation
- **Input Handling**: Read the integers n and k.
- **Lower Bound Calculation**: Compute the minimum value of a as the maximum of 1 and (k - n) to ensure b (k - a) does not exceed n.
- **Upper Bound Calculation**: Compute the maximum value of a as the minimum of (k-1) // 2 (to ensure a < b) and n-1 (to ensure both a and b are within the valid range).
- **Result Calculation**: Check if the upper bound is greater than or equal to the lower bound. If true, the number of valid pairs is the difference between the upper and lower bounds plus one; otherwise, there are no valid pairs.

This approach efficiently narrows down the valid range of possible pairs and ensures the solution is computed in constant time, making it suitable for very large values of n and k up to 10^14.
