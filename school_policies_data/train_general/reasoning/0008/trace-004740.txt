To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimal number of additional marks that Leha needs to add to Noora's existing marks so that her final average rounds up to the highest possible mark, k. 

### Approach
1. **Understand the Rounding Rule**: The final mark is calculated as the average of all marks, rounded to the nearest integer. If the average is exactly halfway between two integers, it rounds up.
2. **Formulate the Condition**: To ensure the final average rounds up to k, the average after adding m marks of value k must be at least k - 0.5. This translates to the inequality:
   \[
   \text{sum\_a} + m \times k \geq (k - 0.5) \times (n + m)
   \]
   where `sum_a` is the sum of the existing marks, n is the number of existing marks, and m is the number of additional marks.
3. **Simplify the Inequality**: By multiplying both sides of the inequality by 2 to avoid floating-point precision issues, we get:
   \[
   2 \times (\text{sum\_a} + m \times k) \geq (2k - 1) \times (n + m)
   \]
4. **Derive the Solution**: Rearrange the inequality to solve for m, leading to the formula:
   \[
   m \geq (2k - 1) \times n - 2 \times \text{sum\_a}
   \]
   The minimal m is the maximum of this value and 0.

### Solution Code
```python
n, k = map(int, input().split())
a = list(map(int, input().split()))
sum_a = sum(a)
required = (2 * k - 1) * n - 2 * sum_a
print(max(0, required))
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The code reads the number of existing marks (n) and the highest possible mark (k), followed by the list of existing marks.
2. **Calculate Sum**: Compute the sum of the existing marks (`sum_a`).
3. **Determine Required Marks**: Using the derived formula, compute the minimal number of additional marks required to ensure the average rounds up to k.
4. **Output Result**: Print the result, ensuring it is non-negative.

This approach efficiently calculates the minimal number of marks using a direct formula, avoiding the need for iterative checks and ensuring optimal performance.
