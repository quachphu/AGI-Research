To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum and maximum number of pairs of friends that could form when participants are split into teams such that each team has at least one member. Each pair of participants in the same team becomes friends, and the number of pairs in a team of size \( k \) is given by \( \binom{k}{2} = \frac{k(k-1)}{2} \).

### Approach
1. **Maximum Number of Pairs**:
   - To maximize the number of pairs, we should form one large team and keep the remaining teams as small as possible (each with 1 member). This configuration maximizes the pairs contributed by the large team. The size of the large team will be \( n - (m - 1) \), leading to \( \binom{n - m + 1}{2} \) pairs.

2. **Minimum Number of Pairs**:
   - To minimize the number of pairs, distribute participants as evenly as possible among the teams. If \( n \) participants are divided into \( m \) teams, the sizes will be either \( \lfloor \frac{n}{m} \rfloor \) or \( \lceil \frac{n}{m} \rceil \). Let \( q = \lfloor \frac{n}{m} \rfloor \) and \( r = n \mod m \). There will be \( r \) teams of size \( q + 1 \) and \( m - r \) teams of size \( q \). The total pairs are calculated by summing the pairs from each team.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    import sys
    n, m = map(int, sys.stdin.readline().split())
    # Calculate maximum pairs
    max_val = (n - m + 1) * (n - m) // 2
    # Calculate minimum pairs
    q, r = divmod(n, m)
    min_val = r * (q + 1) * q // 2 + (m - r) * q * (q - 1) // 2
    print(min_val, max_val)

if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
```

### Explanation
- **Maximum Pairs Calculation**:
  - The formula \((n - m + 1) * (n - m) // 2\) computes the pairs for the largest possible team, which is formed by leaving \( m - 1 \) teams with 1 member each.

- **Minimum Pairs Calculation**:
  - Using integer division and modulo, we split \( n \) participants into \( m \) teams as evenly as possible. The formula \( r * (q + 1) * q // 2 + (m - r) * q * (q - 1) // 2 \) sums the pairs from teams of size \( q + 1 \) and \( q \), where \( r \) is the number of teams with an extra participant.

This approach efficiently computes the required values using basic arithmetic operations, ensuring it handles the large input constraints effectively.
