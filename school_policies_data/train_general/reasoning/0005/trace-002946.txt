To solve this problem, we need to determine how many chips the presenter will have left after distributing them to walruses sitting in a circle. Each walrus receives a number of chips equal to their position in the sequence, and this sequence repeats until the presenter runs out of chips.

### Approach
1. **Understand the Distribution Pattern**: The walruses are numbered from 1 to n, and each walrus receives chips equal to their number in each round. After the nth walrus, the sequence restarts from the first walrus.
2. **Sum of One Full Round**: Calculate the total number of chips required for one complete round (sum of the first n natural numbers).
3. **Determine Full Rounds**: Calculate how many complete rounds can be fully funded with the given chips (m).
4. **Remaining Chips**: After accounting for the full rounds, compute the remaining chips and distribute them in the subsequent partial round until the chips are insufficient for the next walrus.

### Solution Code
```python
n, m = map(int, input().split())
sum_round = n * (n + 1) // 2
k = m // sum_round
remaining = m - k * sum_round

i = 1
while i <= n and remaining >= i:
    remaining -= i
    i += 1

print(remaining)
```

### Explanation
1. **Sum of One Full Round**: The sum of the first n natural numbers is calculated using the formula \( \text{sum\_round} = \frac{n(n + 1)}{2} \).
2. **Full Rounds Calculation**: Determine how many complete rounds can be funded by integer division of the total chips (m) by the sum of one round.
3. **Remaining Chips**: Subtract the chips used in full rounds from the total to get the remaining chips.
4. **Partial Round Distribution**: Distribute the remaining chips starting from the first walrus, reducing the remaining chips by each walrus's number until the remaining chips are insufficient for the next walrus.

This approach efficiently handles the distribution process by leveraging mathematical calculations for full rounds and iterating through the partial round only once, ensuring optimal performance even for the upper constraint limits.
