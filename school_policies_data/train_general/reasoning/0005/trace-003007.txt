To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum number of times Takahashi must perform his action (moving 1 meter north and then turning X degrees counter-clockwise) to return to his starting position. 

### Approach
The key insight is that Takahashi returns to the starting position when the cumulative effect of his movements and turns results in a full rotation (360 degrees). This can be translated into finding the smallest positive integer K such that the total degrees turned (K * X) is a multiple of 360. 

To achieve this, we use the greatest common divisor (gcd) of X and 360. The minimum K is given by dividing 360 by the gcd of X and 360. This is because the gcd helps us determine the smallest number of steps needed to reach a common multiple of both X and 360.

### Solution Code
```python
import math

x = int(input())
g = math.gcd(x, 360)
print(360 // g)
```

### Explanation
1. **Understanding the Rotation**: Each turn of X degrees after moving must eventually sum up to a full rotation (360 degrees) for Takahashi to return to his starting point. This is equivalent to finding the least common multiple (LCM) of X and 360, but adjusted for the problem's specific requirements.
2. **Using GCD**: The problem can be simplified using the gcd of X and 360. The smallest K for which K * X is a multiple of 360 is found by dividing 360 by the gcd of X and 360. This works because the gcd gives the largest number that divides both X and 360 without a remainder, making 360/gcd(X, 360) the smallest number of steps needed.
3. **Efficiency**: The gcd computation is efficient and runs in O(log(min(X, 360))) time, making the solution very efficient even for large values of X within the given constraints.

This approach ensures we find the minimal K quickly and correctly, leveraging mathematical properties of divisors and multiples.
