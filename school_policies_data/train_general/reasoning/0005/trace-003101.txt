To solve this problem, we need to determine the length of the period during which Takahashi can start studying such that he can study for exactly K consecutive minutes without overlapping with his bedtime. 

### Approach
1. **Convert Time to Minutes**: Convert both the wake-up time (H1:M1) and bedtime (H2:M2) into total minutes since midnight. This simplifies the calculation of the total awake time.
2. **Calculate Total Awake Time**: Subtract the wake-up time in minutes from the bedtime in minutes to get the total duration Takahashi is awake.
3. **Determine Study Window**: Subtract the study duration K from the total awake time to find the length of the period during which Takahashi can start studying. This is because Takahashi needs to finish studying by his bedtime, so the latest he can start studying is his bedtime minus K minutes.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    import sys
    H1, M1, H2, M2, K = map(int, sys.stdin.readline().split())
    wake_time = H1 * 60 + M1
    bed_time = H2 * 60 + M2
    total_awake = bed_time - wake_time
    print(total_awake - K)

main()
```

### Explanation
1. **Convert Time to Minutes**: By converting the wake-up time and bedtime into minutes since midnight, we can easily compute the total awake time using simple arithmetic.
2. **Total Awake Time Calculation**: The difference between bedtime and wake-up time in minutes gives the total duration Takahashi is awake.
3. **Adjust for Study Duration**: By subtracting K from the total awake time, we determine the maximum window during which Takahashi can start his study session to ensure it fits entirely within his awake period. This approach efficiently computes the result in constant time, O(1), making it very efficient.
