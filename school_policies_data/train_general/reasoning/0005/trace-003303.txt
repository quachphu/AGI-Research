To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of trailing zeros in the factorial of a given integer \( N \). A trailing zero in a number is produced by multiplying 10, which is the product of 2 and 5. In the prime factorization of a factorial, there are typically more factors of 2 than 5. Therefore, the number of trailing zeros is determined by the number of times 5 is a factor in the numbers from 1 to \( N \).

### Approach
1. **Understanding Factors of 5**: Each multiple of 5 contributes at least one factor of 5. However, numbers like 25 (which is \( 5^2 \)) contribute two factors of 5, numbers like 125 (which is \( 5^3 \)) contribute three factors of 5, and so on.
2. **Counting the Factors**: To count the number of factors of 5 in \( N! \), we repeatedly divide \( N \) by powers of 5 (starting from 5, then 25, 125, etc.) and sum the quotients. This gives the total number of factors of 5 in the prime factorization of \( N! \), which is the number of trailing zeros.

### Solution Code
```python
def count_trailing_zeros():
    n = int(input())
    count = 0
    divisor = 5
    while divisor <= n:
        count += n // divisor
        divisor *= 5
    print(count)

count_trailing_zeros()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The function reads the integer \( N \) from standard input.
2. **Initialization**: We initialize a counter `count` to 0 and start with the divisor set to 5.
3. **Loop Through Powers of 5**: In each iteration of the loop, we divide \( N \) by the current divisor (starting at 5) and add the quotient to the count. The divisor is then multiplied by 5 to move to the next higher power of 5 (25, 125, etc.).
4. **Termination**: The loop terminates when the divisor exceeds \( N \), ensuring we do not process higher powers of 5 unnecessarily.
5. **Output**: Finally, the accumulated count is printed, which represents the number of trailing zeros in \( N! \).

This approach efficiently counts the factors of 5 in the prime factorization of \( N! \) using a logarithmic number of iterations relative to \( N \), making it both time and space efficient.
