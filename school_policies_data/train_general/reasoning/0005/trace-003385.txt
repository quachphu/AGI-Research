To solve this problem, we need to determine the maximum number of people in a queue who will not be disappointed if we rearrange the order in which they are served. A person is disappointed if the total waiting time before they are served exceeds their service time.

### Approach
The key insight is to sort the service times in ascending order and then use a greedy algorithm to select the maximum number of people such that each person's service time is at least the sum of the service times of all previously selected people. This approach ensures that we minimize the growth of the waiting time, allowing more people to be included in the queue without being disappointed.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
times = list(map(int, input().split()))
times.sort()
current_sum = 0
count = 0
for t in times:
    if current_sum <= t:
        count += 1
        current_sum += t
print(count)
```

### Explanation
1. **Sorting the Service Times**: By sorting the service times in ascending order, we start with the smallest possible service times. This helps in keeping the cumulative waiting time as low as possible initially, allowing more subsequent people to be included.
2. **Greedy Selection**: We iterate through the sorted list and maintain a running sum of the service times of the people we have included so far. For each person, if their service time is greater than or equal to the current running sum, they are included in the queue. This ensures that their waiting time (sum of previous service times) does not exceed their service time.
3. **Counting Non-disappointed People**: Each time a person is included in the queue, we increment the count of non-disappointed people and update the running sum of service times.

This approach efficiently ensures that we maximize the number of non-disappointed people with a time complexity of O(n log n) due to the sorting step, followed by a linear scan through the sorted list.
