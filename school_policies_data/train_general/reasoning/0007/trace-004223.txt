To solve this problem, we need to determine if a given number \( N \) is an Asmany number. An Asmany number for length \( L \) is the count of binary strings of length \( L \) that have an equal number of "00" and "11" substrings. The solution involves checking if \( N \) can be represented as a specific binomial coefficient multiplied by 2.

### Approach
1. **Check Evenness**: Asmany numbers are always even, so if \( N \) is odd, the answer is immediately "NO".
2. **Compute \( M \)**: For even \( N \), compute \( M = N / 2 \).
3. **Check Sequences**: Determine if \( M \) can be represented as a term in one of two sequences derived from binomial coefficients:
   - **Even Sequence**: Terms are of the form \( C(2k-2, k-1) \) for some \( k \).
   - **Odd Sequence**: Terms are of the form \( C(2k-1, k) \) for some \( k \).
