To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum amount of money Igor will spend to buy all items, given that he must buy at least `k` items immediately and can wait to buy the rest after a week when their prices might be lower. The goal is to minimize the total expenditure by strategically choosing which items to buy now and which to buy later.

### Approach
1. **Categorize Items**: Split the items into two groups based on their current and post-discount prices:
   - **Group A**: Items where buying now is cheaper (current price `ai` is less than post-discount price `bi`).
   - **Group B**: Items where buying later is cheaper or the same (current price `ai` is greater than or equal to post-discount price `bi`).

2. **Calculate Initial Sums**:
   - Sum the prices of all items in Group A bought immediately.
   - Sum the post-discount prices of all items in Group B bought later.

3. **Check Minimum Requirement**:
   - If the number of items in Group A is at least `k`, Igor meets the requirement by buying all items in Group A now. The total cost is the sum of Group A prices and Group B post-discount prices.
   - If the number of items in Group A is less than `k`, Igor must buy additional items from Group B now. Choose the items from Group B with the smallest difference between their current and post-discount prices to minimize the extra cost incurred.

### Solution Code
```python
n, k = map(int, input().split())
a = list(map(int, input().split()))
b = list(map(int, input().split()))

sum_a = 0
sum_b = 0
diff_list = []

for ai, bi in zip(a, b):
    if ai < bi:
        sum_a += ai
    else:
        sum_b += bi
        diff_list.append(ai - bi)

m = n - len(diff_list)

if m >= k:
    print(sum_a + sum_b)
else:
    need = k - m
    diff_list.sort()
    sum_extra = sum(diff_list[:need])
    print(sum_a + sum_b + sum_extra)
```

### Explanation
1. **Categorizing Items**: We iterate through each item and check if buying now is cheaper. If so, we add its price to `sum_a`. Otherwise, we add its post-discount price to `sum_b` and note the price difference for potential later use.
2. **Handling Minimum Requirement**: If the number of items in Group A (`m`) is sufficient to meet the requirement `k`, we directly sum their prices. If not, we need to buy additional items from Group B. By sorting the price differences and selecting the smallest ones, we ensure that the extra cost incurred is minimized.
3. **Efficiency**: Sorting the differences of Group B items ensures we efficiently find the least costly items to buy now, leading to an optimal solution with a time complexity of O(n log n) due to the sorting step.

This approach ensures that Igor spends the minimum possible amount by leveraging the best prices available now and later, while adhering to the constraint of buying at least `k` items immediately.
