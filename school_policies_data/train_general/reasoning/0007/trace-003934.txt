To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimal number of jumps Jack requires to reach a specific point on a number line starting from zero. Each jump increases by one unit in length, and Jack can jump either left or right. The key is to find the smallest number of jumps such that the sum of jumps (considering direction changes) equals the target point.

### Approach
1. **Problem Analysis**:
   - Jack starts at point 0 and needs to reach a target point `x`.
   - Each jump increases by 1 unit, and he can jump left or right.
   - The sum of the first `n` jumps is `S(n) = n*(n+1)/2`.
   - The goal is to find the smallest `n` such that `S(n) >= |x|` and `(S(n) - |x|)` is even. This ensures that the difference can be adjusted by flipping the direction of some jumps.

2. **Key Insights**:
   - Convert the target to its absolute value since the direction can be adjusted by flipping jumps.
   - Use the quadratic formula to find the smallest `n` such that `S(n) >= target`.
   - Check if the difference `S(n) - target` is even. If not, adjust `n` by 1 or 2 based on parity checks to ensure the difference becomes even.

3. **Algorithm**:
   - Compute the smallest `n` using the quadratic formula.
   - Adjust `n` if necessary to ensure `S(n) >= target`.
   - Check the parity of the difference. If even, return `n`. Otherwise, adjust `n` based on its parity to make the difference even.
