To solve this problem, we need to determine the maximum possible distance between two rivaling students in a row after performing at most a given number of adjacent swaps. The approach involves calculating the initial distance between the two students and then determining how much this distance can be increased by moving each student towards the ends of the row.

### Approach
1. **Initial Setup**: For each test case, read the number of students `n`, the maximum swaps allowed `x`, and the positions of the two students `a` and `b`.
2. **Initial Distance**: Calculate the initial distance between the two students by taking the absolute difference of their positions.
3. **Maximize Distance**: Determine the maximum possible steps each student can move towards the ends of the row. The left student can move towards the start (position 1) and the right student can move towards the end (position `n`).
4. **Calculate Available Steps**: Compute the total steps available to move both students towards their respective ends. This is the sum of the steps the left student can move left and the steps the right student can move right.
5. **Determine Effective Swaps**: The effective number of swaps is the minimum of the available steps and the allowed swaps `x`.
6. **Compute Maximum Distance**: The maximum possible distance is the initial distance plus the effective swaps.

### Solution Code
```python
t = int(input())
for _ in range(t):
    n, x, a, b = map(int, input().split())
    min_pos = min(a, b)
    max_pos = max(a, b)
    original_distance = max_pos - min_pos
    steps_available = (min_pos - 1) + (n - max_pos)
    added = min(x, steps_available)
    print(original_distance + added)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The code reads the number of test cases and iterates through each case.
2. **Initial Positions**: The positions of the two students are sorted to determine the leftmost (`min_pos`) and rightmost (`max_pos`) positions.
3. **Initial Distance Calculation**: The distance between the two students is computed as the difference between `max_pos` and `min_pos`.
4. **Available Movement Steps**: The steps each student can move towards their respective ends are calculated. The left student can move `min_pos - 1` steps left, and the right student can move `n - max_pos` steps right.
5. **Effective Swaps**: The effective number of swaps that can be used to increase the distance is the minimum of the allowed swaps `x` and the total available steps.
6. **Result Calculation**: The final result is the initial distance plus the effective swaps, which gives the maximum possible distance after the allowed swaps.

This approach ensures that we efficiently compute the maximum possible distance by leveraging the allowed swaps optimally, considering the constraints and movement possibilities.
