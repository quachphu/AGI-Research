To solve this problem, we need to form the maximum number of teams of exactly three students from given groups of students. Each group can either be a single student or a pair of students. The key challenge is to determine how to optimally combine these groups to form the maximum number of teams.

### Approach
1. **Identify Group Types**: Count the number of groups that contain a single student (ones) and the number of groups that contain two students (twos).
2. **Formulate Team Combinations**: There are two valid team formations:
   - A team consisting of one group of two students and one group of one student (2 + 1).
   - A team consisting of three groups of one student each (1 + 1 + 1).
3. **Optimize Team Formation**: The optimal solution involves maximizing the number of teams formed by the first type (2 + 1) as much as possible, then using the remaining single students to form teams of the second type (1 + 1 + 1).

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
a = list(map(int, input().split()))
ones = a.count(1)
twos = a.count(2)
max_teams = (min(ones, twos) * 2 + ones) // 3
print(max_teams)
```

### Explanation
1. **Count Groups**: We first count the number of groups with one student (`ones`) and the number of groups with two students (`twos`).
2. **Calculate Maximum Teams**: The formula `(min(ones, twos) * 2 + ones) // 3` is derived to find the maximum number of teams:
   - `min(ones, twos)` gives the maximum number of (2 + 1) teams we can form.
   - Each (2 + 1) team uses one group of two students and one group of one student.
   - After forming these teams, the remaining single students are used to form (1 + 1 + 1) teams.
   - The formula efficiently combines both types of teams to maximize the total count.

This approach ensures that we use the optimal combination of group types to form the maximum number of teams possible, considering both constraints and possible team configurations.
