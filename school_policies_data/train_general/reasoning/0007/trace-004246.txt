To solve this problem, we need to determine if Kirill can buy a potion with an exact efficiency value of \( k \) from a store where each potion is characterized by its experience and cost. The efficiency of a potion is the ratio of experience to cost. We need to check if there exists at least one potion such that this ratio equals \( k \).

### Approach
1. **Understand the Problem Requirements**: We need to find integers \( a \) (experience) and \( b \) (cost) such that \( a/b = k \), where \( a \) lies within the range \([l, r]\) and \( b \) lies within the range \([x, y]\).

2. **Formulate the Equation**: The equation \( a = k \times b \) must hold true. Here, \( a \) must be an integer and lie within \([l, r]\), and \( b \) must be an integer within \([x, y]\).

3. **Determine Valid Ranges**:
   - Calculate the minimum possible value for \( a \) as \( k \times x \).
   - Calculate the maximum possible value for \( a \) as \( k \times y \).
   - The valid range for \( a \) must intersect with the range \([l, r]\).

4. **Check for Valid Multiples**: If the intersection of the ranges \([l, r]\) and \([k \times x, k \times y]\) is non-empty, check if there exists any multiple of \( k \) within this intersection. This ensures that there is a valid \( a \) such that \( a = k \times b \) and \( b \) is within \([x, y]\).

### Solution Code
```python
l, r, x, y, k = map(int, input().split())
lower_a = max(l, k * x)
upper_a = min(r, k * y)

if lower_a > upper_a:
    print("NO")
else:
    # Calculate the smallest multiple of k >= lower_a
    quotient = (lower_a + k - 1) // k
    first_multiple = quotient * k
    print("YES" if first_multiple <= upper_a else "NO")
```

### Explanation
1. **Input Reading**: The input values are read and parsed into integers \( l, r, x, y, k \).

2. **Range Calculation**:
   - `lower_a` is the maximum of the lower bound of experience \( l \) and the minimum possible experience derived from the cost range \( k \times x \).
   - `upper_a` is the minimum of the upper bound of experience \( r \) and the maximum possible experience derived from the cost range \( k \times y \).

3. **Range Check**: If the calculated lower bound of experience exceeds the upper bound, it immediately indicates no valid potion exists, resulting in "NO".

4. **Multiple Check**: Using integer arithmetic, we determine the smallest multiple of \( k \) that is greater than or equal to `lower_a`. If this multiple is within the valid range `[lower_a, upper_a]`, the answer is "YES", otherwise "NO".

This approach efficiently checks the existence of valid experience and cost pairs without iterating through all possible values, ensuring optimal performance even for large input ranges.
