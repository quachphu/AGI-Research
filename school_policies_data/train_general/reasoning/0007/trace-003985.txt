To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum number of moves Nastya has to make to collect all the coins from the manholes, considering she can move left or right, throw stones from her current position to any other manhole, and open a manhole only if it has no stones.

### Approach
The key insight is to recognize that the optimal strategy involves minimizing both the movement cost and the number of stone throws. Nastya should process the manholes in such a way that minimizes the total distance traveled. This can be achieved by either processing all manholes to the left first and then all to the right, or vice versa. The movement cost is determined by the minimal path between these two strategies.

### Solution Code
```python
n, k = map(int, input().split())
option1 = (k - 1) + (n - 1)
option2 = (n - k) + (n - 1)
movement_cost = min(option1, option2)
total_steps = 2 * n + 1 + movement_cost
print(total_steps)
```

### Explanation
1. **Movement Cost Calculation**:
   - **Option 1**: Process all manholes to the left first, then move to the rightmost manhole. This results in a movement cost of `(k - 1) + (n - 1)`.
   - **Option 2**: Process all manholes to the right first, then move to the leftmost manhole. This results in a movement cost of `(n - k) + (n - 1)`.
   - The minimal movement cost is the smaller value between these two options.

2. **Total Steps Calculation**:
   - **Stone Throws and Opens**: Each manhole requires 1 move to throw its stone and 1 move to open it, leading to a total of `2 * n` moves. Additionally, there is an extra move due to the initial stone throw strategy.
   - Adding the minimal movement cost to this gives the total number of moves required.

This approach ensures that we efficiently minimize both the movement and stone-throwing steps, leading to the optimal solution.
