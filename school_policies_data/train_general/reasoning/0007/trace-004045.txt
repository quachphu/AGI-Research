The solution involves converting the given time into total minutes, adding the passed minutes, and then converting the result back into the 24-hour format.

### Approach
1. **Parse Input**: Read the current time and the number of minutes passed.
2. **Convert Time to Minutes**: Convert the current time into total minutes since midnight.
3. **Calculate New Time**: Add the passed minutes to the total minutes and handle overflow by using modulo 1440 (the number of minutes in a day).
4. **Convert Back to Hours and Minutes**: Convert the total minutes back into hours and minutes.
5. **Format Output**: Ensure the hours and minutes are formatted with leading zeros if necessary.

### Solution Code
```python
def calculate_new_time():
    current_time = input().strip()
    a = int(input())
    h, m = map(int, current_time.split(':'))
    total = h * 60 + m + a
    total %= 1440
    new_h = total // 60
    new_m = total % 60
    print(f"{new_h:02d}:{new_m:02d}")

calculate_new_time()
```

### Explanation
1. **Parsing Input**: The input is read as two lines. The first line is the current time in "hh:mm" format, and the second line is the number of minutes passed.
2. **Conversion to Minutes**: The current time is split into hours and minutes, which are then converted into total minutes since midnight.
3. **Adding Minutes and Handling Overflow**: The total minutes after adding the passed minutes are computed. Using modulo 1440 ensures that the time wraps around correctly if it exceeds a full day.
4. **Conversion Back to Hours and Minutes**: The total minutes are converted back into hours and minutes. The hours are obtained by integer division by 60, and the remaining minutes are obtained using modulo 60.
5. **Formatting**: The hours and minutes are formatted to ensure they are two digits long, with leading zeros if necessary, using Python's formatted string literals.

This approach efficiently handles all edge cases, including times that wrap around midnight, and ensures the correct format for the output.
