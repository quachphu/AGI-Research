To solve this problem, we need to redistribute tasks among students such that the total time spent by all students is minimized. Each student's laziness level is equal to the difficulty of their initial task. The goal is to find the optimal permutation of tasks that minimizes the total time, which is calculated as the sum of the product of each student's laziness and the difficulty of the task they are assigned.

### Approach
The key insight is derived from the rearrangement inequality, which states that the sum of products of two sequences is minimized when one sequence is sorted in ascending order and the other in descending order. By applying this principle, we can achieve the minimum total time by:
1. Sorting the list of tasks (and student laziness levels) in ascending order.
2. Sorting another copy of the list in descending order.
3. Pairing the elements of these two sorted lists and summing their products.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
a = [int(input()) for _ in range(n)]
sorted_asc = sorted(a)
sorted_desc = sorted(a, reverse=True)
total = sum(x * y for x, y in zip(sorted_asc, sorted_desc)) % 10007
print(total)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input:** The input consists of an integer `n` followed by `n` integers representing both the students' laziness levels and the tasks' difficulties.
2. **Sorting:** We sort the list of integers in ascending order to get `sorted_asc` and in descending order to get `sorted_desc`.
3. **Calculating Total Time:** We compute the total time by multiplying corresponding elements from `sorted_asc` and `sorted_desc` and summing these products. This approach leverages the rearrangement inequality to ensure the sum is minimized.
4. **Modulo Operation:** The result is taken modulo 10007 to meet the problem's requirement.

This approach ensures that the solution is efficient and optimal, with a time complexity dominated by the sorting step, which is O(n log n). This efficiently handles the upper constraint of n being up to 100,000.
