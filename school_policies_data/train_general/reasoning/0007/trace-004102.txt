To solve this problem, we need to determine the smallest integer \( x \) that is greater than a given integer \( n \) and divisible by another given integer \( k \). 

### Approach
The key insight to solve this problem efficiently is to recognize that the smallest integer \( x \) greater than \( n \) and divisible by \( k \) can be derived using integer division. Here’s the step-by-step approach:

1. **Integer Division**: Compute the quotient when \( n \) is divided by \( k \). This quotient tells us how many times \( k \) fits into \( n \).
2. **Adjust the Quotient**: If \( n \) is exactly divisible by \( k \), the next multiple of \( k \) will be one more than the quotient. If \( n \) is not exactly divisible by \( k \), the next multiple will still be one more than the quotient. This adjustment ensures that we always get the next multiple of \( k \) greater than \( n \).
3. **Calculate the Result**: Multiply the adjusted quotient by \( k \) to get the desired result.

### Solution Code
```python
n, k = map(int, input().split())
x = ((n // k) + 1) * k
print(x)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of two integers \( n \) and \( k \).
2. **Compute Quotient**: Using integer division \( n // k \), we determine how many times \( k \) can fit into \( n \).
3. **Adjust Quotient**: By adding 1 to the quotient, we ensure that we move to the next multiple of \( k \).
4. **Calculate Result**: Multiplying the adjusted quotient by \( k \) gives the smallest integer greater than \( n \) that is divisible by \( k \).

This approach efficiently computes the result in constant time \( O(1) \), making it suitable for large values of \( n \) and \( k \) up to \( 10^9 \).
