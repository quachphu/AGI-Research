To solve this problem, we need to determine the time required for all matchsticks to burn completely when the free ends of a specific range of matchsticks are lit. The solution involves efficiently calculating the minimum and maximum values within specific ranges using segment trees to handle the constraints efficiently.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Burning Process**:
   - Each matchstick burns from the front end when lit.
   - When any matchstick burns completely from the front, the rear end (common to all matchsticks) is ignited. This causes all matchsticks to start burning from the rear end as well.
   - The time taken for a matchstick to burn completely when lit from both ends is half the time required when lit from one end.

2. **Key Observations**:
   - The rear end is ignited at the minimum burn time of the lit matchsticks.
   - For matchsticks within the lit range, the burn time is the average of their individual burn time and the minimum burn time of the range.
   - For matchsticks outside the lit range, the burn time is the sum of the minimum burn time (when the rear end ignites) and their individual burn time.

3. **Efficient Range Queries**:
   - Use segment trees to efficiently compute the minimum and maximum values within ranges. This allows us to handle up to 100,000 queries efficiently.
