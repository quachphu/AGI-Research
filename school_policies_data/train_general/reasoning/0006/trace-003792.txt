### Approach
1. **Understanding Lucky Numbers**: A lucky number must contain the digit \( d \). For example, if \( d = 7 \), numbers like 7, 17, 70, etc., are considered lucky.
2. **Key Insight**: For a number \( a_i \) to be expressible as a sum of lucky numbers, it must be possible to find at least one lucky number \( x \) such that \( x \leq a_i \) and \( x \mod d = a_i \mod d \). This allows the remaining part of \( a_i \) (after subtracting \( x \)) to be formed by summing multiples of \( d \), which are also lucky numbers.
3. **Precomputation**: For each possible remainder when divided by \( d \) (from 0 to \( d-1 \)), precompute the smallest lucky number that has that remainder. This helps in quickly determining if a valid \( x \) exists for any given \( a_i \).
