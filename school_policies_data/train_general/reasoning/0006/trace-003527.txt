To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum number of seconds required for two people to cut a candy bar into pieces such that each person gets exactly half the length of the candy. The key challenge is to minimize the sum of the cutting times at specific positions.

### Approach
1. **Single Cut Check**: Check if cutting the candy at the exact middle position (N/2) is feasible. This cut would split the candy into two equal parts, and the cost is the time required to cut at this middle position.
2. **Pair of Cuts Check**: Check all possible pairs of cuts where the sum of the lengths of the segments formed by these cuts equals half the length of the candy. Specifically, for each possible starting cut position `i`, determine the ending cut position `j` such that the segments from the start to `i` and from `j` to the end sum up to half the candy length. The cost for each valid pair of cuts is the sum of their individual cutting times.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
t = [int(input()) for _ in range(n-1)]
min_cost = float('inf')
half = n // 2

# Check single cut at the middle position
if 1 <= half <= n-1:
    min_cost = t[half - 1]

# Check pairs of cuts i and j where j = i + half
for i in range(1, n - half + 1):
    j = i + half
    if j > n - 1:
        continue
    current_cost = t[i - 1] + t[j - 1]
    if current_cost < min_cost:
        min_cost = current_cost

print(min_cost)
```

### Explanation
1. **Single Cut Check**: The candy can be split into two equal parts by making a single cut at the middle position (N/2). If this position is valid (within the bounds of the candy), the cost is the time required to make this cut.
2. **Pair of Cuts Check**: For each starting position `i`, calculate the corresponding ending position `j` such that the sum of the segments from the start to `i` and from `j` to the end equals half the length of the candy. The cost for each valid pair of cuts (i.e., both positions are valid) is the sum of their respective cutting times. The minimum cost from all valid pairs is determined.

This approach efficiently checks both single and pairs of cuts to find the optimal solution with minimal computational complexity, making it suitable for large values of N up to 10,000.
