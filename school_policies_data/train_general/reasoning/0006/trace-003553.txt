To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum time Vasya needs to reach a specific house on a street where houses are arranged in a particular order. The houses on one side of the street are numbered with odd numbers in ascending order from the start, while the houses on the opposite side are numbered with even numbers in descending order from the end. The key insight is that each house and its directly opposite house have the same minimum time to reach from the starting point.

### Approach
1. **Understanding House Arrangement**: Odd-numbered houses are arranged from the start of the street, while even-numbered houses are arranged from the end. Each odd-numbered house has a corresponding even-numbered house directly opposite it.
2. **Time Calculation**: 
   - For an odd-numbered house `a`, the time to reach it is calculated by `(a + 1) // 2` because each step between consecutive odd houses takes 1 second.
   - For an even-numbered house `a`, the time is calculated by `(n - a) // 2 + 1` because the even houses are counted from the end of the street, and each step between consecutive even houses also takes 1 second.

### Solution Code
```python
n, a = map(int, input().split())
if a % 2 == 0:
    print((n - a) // 2 + 1)
else:
    print((a + 1) // 2)
```

### Explanation
- **Odd-numbered Houses**: The time to reach an odd-numbered house `a` is determined by its position in the sequence of odd houses. For example, house 5 is the third odd house (1, 3, 5), so it takes `(5 + 1) // 2 = 3` seconds.
- **Even-numbered Houses**: The time to reach an even-numbered house `a` is determined by its position from the end of the street. For example, house 4 in a street with `n = 6` is the second even house from the end (6, 4), so it takes `(6 - 4) // 2 + 1 = 2` seconds.

This approach efficiently computes the minimum time by leveraging the symmetrical arrangement of the houses, ensuring that each house and its opposite have the same time calculation. This solution runs in constant time O(1), making it very efficient even for large values of `n` up to 100,000.
