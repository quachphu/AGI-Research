### Approach
1. **Direct Equality Check**: If \( x \) and \( y \) are equal, \( x^y \) will obviously be equal to \( y^x \).
2. **Special Cases for 1**: If either \( x \) or \( y \) is 1, we can directly determine the result since \( 1^y = 1 \) and \( y^1 = y \).
3. **Known Pairs**: The pairs (2, 4) and (4, 2) are known to be equal, so we handle them separately.
4. **Logarithmic Comparison**: For other cases, we compare the natural logarithms of \( x^y \) and \( y^x \). This reduces the problem to comparing \( y \cdot \ln(x) \) and \( x \cdot \ln(y) \). Using high-precision arithmetic ensures accuracy even when the values are very close.
