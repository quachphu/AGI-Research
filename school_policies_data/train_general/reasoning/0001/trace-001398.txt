To solve this problem, we need to determine the greatest common divisor (GCD) of the factorials of two given integers A and B. The key insight here is that the GCD of two factorials is simply the factorial of the smaller of the two integers, since the smaller factorial will always be a divisor of the larger factorial.

### Approach
1. **Read Input:** Read the two integers A and B from the input.
2. **Determine the Minimum:** Identify the smaller of the two integers, as the GCD of their factorials will be the factorial of this smaller integer.
3. **Compute Factorial:** Calculate the factorial of the smaller integer. This can be done efficiently using a loop to multiply all integers from 1 up to the smaller integer.

### Solution Code
```python
def compute_gcd_factorial():
    import sys
    a, b = map(int, sys.stdin.readline().split())
    m = min(a, b)
    factorial = 1
    for i in range(1, m + 1):
        factorial *= i
    print(factorial)

compute_gcd_factorial()
```

### Explanation
- **Reading Input:** The integers A and B are read from standard input.
- **Finding the Minimum:** The minimum of the two integers is determined using the `min` function.
- **Factorial Calculation:** The factorial of the minimum integer is computed by iterating from 1 to the minimum integer and multiplying the values together.
- **Output the Result:** The computed factorial, which is the GCD of A! and B!, is printed.

This approach efficiently computes the required result by leveraging the mathematical property that the factorial of the smaller number is a divisor of the factorial of the larger number. The time complexity is O(min(A, B)), which is efficient given the constraint that the minimum value can be at most 12. This ensures the solution is both optimal and easy to understand.
