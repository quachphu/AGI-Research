### Approach
The key insight is that \( f(f(x)) \) effectively strips \( x \) of its trailing zeros. Therefore, \( g(x) \) simplifies to \( 10^m \), where \( m \) is the number of trailing zeros in \( x \). The number of distinct values of \( g(x) \) is thus the number of possible values of \( m \) such that there exists some \( x \leq n \) with exactly \( m \) trailing zeros.

To determine this, we need to find the maximum number of trailing zeros \( m \) such that \( 10^m \leq n \). This can be derived by considering the length of \( n \) and comparing it to the smallest number with \( m \) trailing zeros, which is \( 10^m \).
