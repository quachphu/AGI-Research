To solve this problem, we need to determine the correct choice from three options (1, 2, and 3) given that two of them are incorrect. The solution involves identifying the remaining number that is neither of the two incorrect choices.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Problem**: We are given two distinct integers, A and B, each of which can be 1, 2, or 3. These two numbers are incorrect choices, so the correct answer must be the third number that is not A or B.
2. **Sum Approach**: The sum of the numbers 1, 2, and 3 is 6. By subtracting the values of A and B from this sum, we can directly find the remaining number, which is the correct choice. This approach works because A and B are always distinct and valid (each being one of 1, 2, or 3), ensuring that their sum subtracted from 6 will yield the correct third number.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    a = int(input())
    b = int(input())
    print(6 - a - b)

main()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The function reads two integers from the standard input. These integers represent the incorrect choices A and B.
2. **Calculating the Correct Choice**: By subtracting the sum of A and B from 6, we determine the remaining number which is the correct choice. This method leverages the fixed sum of the three possible values (1 + 2 + 3 = 6) to efficiently find the solution.
3. **Output the Result**: The result is printed as the correct choice.

This approach is efficient with a time complexity of O(1) since it involves only a constant amount of arithmetic operations and input/output operations. The solution is optimal and straightforward, ensuring correctness by leveraging the properties of the problem constraints.
