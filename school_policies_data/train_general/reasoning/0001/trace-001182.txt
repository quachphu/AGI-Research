To solve this problem, we need to determine the area of the smallest circular plot that can enclose a triangular house with given side lengths. The smallest such circle is the circumcircle of the triangle, and its area can be calculated using the circumradius of the triangle.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Circumradius**: The circumradius (R) of a triangle with sides A, B, and C can be calculated using the formula \( R = \frac{A \cdot B \cdot C}{4 \cdot \Delta} \), where \( \Delta \) is the area of the triangle.
2. **Area of the Triangle**: The area \( \Delta \) can be computed using Heron's formula, which is \( \Delta = \sqrt{s \cdot (s - A) \cdot (s - B) \cdot (s - C)} \), where \( s \) is the semi-perimeter of the triangle \( s = \frac{A + B + C}{2} \).
3. **Area of the Circumcircle**: Once the circumradius is known, the area of the circumcircle is \( \pi \cdot R^2 \).

### Solution Code
```python
import math

pi = 3.1415

def compute_area():
    import sys
    input = sys.stdin.read().split()
    T = int(input[0])
    idx = 1
    for _ in range(T):
        A = float(input[idx])
        B = float(input[idx+1])
        C = float(input[idx+2])
        idx += 3
        a, b, c = A, B, C
        s = (a + b + c) / 2
        area_triangle = math.sqrt(s * (s - a) * (s - b) * (s - c))
        R = (a * b * c) / (4 * area_triangle)
        area_circle = pi * (R ** 2)
        print("{0:.4f}".format(area_circle))

compute_area()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input is read all at once and split into a list for efficient processing.
2. **Loop Through Test Cases**: For each test case, the side lengths A, B, and C are read.
3. **Calculate Semi-Perimeter and Area**: Using Heron's formula, the semi-perimeter and area of the triangle are computed.
4. **Compute Circumradius**: The circumradius is derived from the product of the sides divided by four times the area of the triangle.
5. **Calculate and Print Result**: The area of the circumcircle is calculated using the circumradius and printed to four decimal places.

This approach efficiently computes the required area for each test case, ensuring accuracy and precision as required by the problem constraints.
