To solve this problem, we need to determine the weight of the minimum spanning tree (MST) of a complete graph where each edge between vertices \(u\) and \(v\) has a weight equal to \(u \oplus v\) (bitwise XOR). The solution must efficiently handle very large values of \(n\) (up to \(10^{12}\)).

### Approach
The key insight is that the MST can be constructed by connecting each vertex to the tree using the smallest possible edges, which are determined by the least significant bit (LSB) of their indices. The sum of the weights of these edges forms the MST. 

The approach involves:
1. Recognizing that the weight of the MST is the sum of the LSB values of all integers from 1 to \(n-1\).
2. Using a mathematical formula to compute this sum efficiently by iterating through the powers of 2 and summing their contributions based on their positions in the binary representation of \(n-1\).

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
m = n - 1
total = m
k = 1
while True:
    two_power_k = 1 << k  # Equivalent to 2^k
    if two_power_k > m:
        break
    quotient = m // two_power_k
    term = quotient * (two_power_k >> 1)  # 2^(k-1)
    total += term
    k += 1
print(total)
```

1. **Input Handling**: Read the integer \(n\) which represents the number of vertices.
2. **Initialization**: Compute \(m = n - 1\) which is the maximum vertex index. Initialize the total sum with \(m\).
3. **Iterate Over Powers of 2**: For each power of 2 (starting from \(2^1\)), compute how many times this power contributes to the sum by considering the quotient when \(m\) is divided by this power. Multiply this quotient by half the power to get the contribution of this power to the total sum.
4. **Termination Condition**: Stop iterating when the current power of 2 exceeds \(m\).
5. **Output the Result**: Print the computed total sum, which is the weight of the MST.

This approach efficiently calculates the sum using bitwise operations and mathematical insights, ensuring it runs in \(O(\log n)\) time complexity, which is feasible even for very large values of \(n\).
