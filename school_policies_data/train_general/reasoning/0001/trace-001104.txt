To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of valid ways to assign numbers to the plaques of houses such that the given conditions are met. The solution involves combinatorial mathematics and modular arithmetic to efficiently compute the result.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Conditions**: 
   - Any house from 1 to k must eventually lead to house 1.
   - Any house from k+1 to n must not lead to house 1.
   - House 1 must form a cycle that allows it to return to itself.

2. **Combinatorial Insight**:
   - For houses 1 to k, each house must form a structure where all paths eventually lead to house 1. This can be modeled as a rooted tree where each node points to another node, forming a cycle. The number of such valid structures is given by \( k^{k-1} \).
   - For houses k+1 to n, each house must form a structure that does not connect to house 1. This is simply \( (n-k)^{n-k} \) since each house can point to any other house in the same range.

3. **Modular Arithmetic**:
   - The results can be large, so we use modulo \( 10^9 + 7 \) to keep the numbers manageable.

print(ans)
