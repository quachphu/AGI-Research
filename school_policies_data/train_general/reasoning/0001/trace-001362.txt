The solution involves factorizing each number into its prime factors and using these factorizations to avoid conflicts where the product of two numbers forms a cube.

### Approach
1. **Prime Factorization**: For each number, factorize it into its prime factors and reduce each exponent modulo 3. This gives a "reduced form" where each prime factor's exponent is either 0, 1, or 2. Exponents of 0 are discarded as they do not affect the cubic nature of the product.
2. **Complementary Pairs**: For each reduced form, compute its complement. The complement of a reduced form ensures that when multiplied with the original form, the result is a cube. For example, if a number has a prime factor with exponent 1, its complement will have an exponent of 2 for the same prime.
3. **Frequency Counting**: Count the occurrences of each reduced form. Use these counts to determine the maximum number of non-conflicting integers by comparing each form with its complement and taking the maximum count from each pair.
