To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum positive integer \( k \) such that applying the function \( f \) \( k \) times results in an idempotent function. A function \( g \) is idempotent if applying it twice yields the same result as applying it once, i.e., \( g(g(x)) = g(x) \) for all \( x \).

### Approach
1. **Understanding Idempotency**: For a function \( g \) to be idempotent, applying it twice must be the same as applying it once. This means that after \( k \) applications of \( f \), the resulting function \( f^k \) must satisfy \( f^k(f^k(x)) = f^k(x) \) for all \( x \).

2. **Iterative Application**: We will iteratively check for each \( k \) starting from 1. For each \( k \), compute the function \( f^k \) by applying \( f \) \( k \) times to each element. Then, check if applying \( f^k \) again (i.e., \( f^{2k} \)) results in the same values as \( f^k \).

3. **Efficiency Considerations**: Given the constraints (n up to 200), iteratively applying \( f \) up to a reasonable limit (e.g., until the idempotent condition is met) is feasible. For each \( k \), we compute \( f^k \) and then verify the idempotent condition.
