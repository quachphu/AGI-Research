To solve this problem, we need to determine the maximum possible difference between the maximum and minimum amounts of water in barrels after performing at most `k` pour operations. Each pour operation allows transferring any amount of water from one barrel to another.

### Approach
The key insight is that to maximize the difference between the maximum and minimum water amounts, we should concentrate as much water as possible into one barrel while leaving others empty. Here's the detailed approach:

1. **Sort the Barrels**: Sort the barrels in descending order of their water contents. This allows us to easily access the barrels with the most water.
2. **Sum the Largest (k+1) Barrels**: The maximum amount of water we can concentrate into a single barrel is the sum of the `k+1` largest barrels. This is because each of the `k` pour operations can transfer the entire contents of one of the next `k` largest barrels into the largest barrel.
3. **Calculate the Difference**: The minimum value in the barrels after these operations will be zero (since we pour out all the water from `k` barrels). Thus, the difference is simply the sum of the `k+1` largest barrels.

### Solution Code
```python
t = int(input())
for _ in range(t):
    n, k = map(int, input().split())
    a = list(map(int, input().split()))
    a.sort(reverse=True)
    print(sum(a[:k+1]))
```

### Explanation
1. **Input Reading**: The code reads the number of test cases `t`, then for each test case, reads `n` (number of barrels) and `k` (number of allowed pour operations), followed by the list of water amounts in the barrels.
2. **Sorting**: The list of water amounts is sorted in descending order to easily access the largest elements.
3. **Summing the Largest Elements**: The sum of the first `k+1` elements from the sorted list is computed. This sum represents the maximum possible water concentration in a single barrel after `k` pour operations.
4. **Output**: The result for each test case is printed, which is the sum of the largest `k+1` elements.

This approach efficiently computes the desired maximum difference using sorting and summation, ensuring optimal performance even for large input sizes.
