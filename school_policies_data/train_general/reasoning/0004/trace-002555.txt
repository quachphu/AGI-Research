To solve this problem, we need to determine the maximum sum of money Bob can earn by taking up to `m` TV sets from a sale where some TV sets have negative prices (meaning their owners are willing to pay Bob to take them). The goal is to maximize Bob's earnings by selecting the optimal combination of TV sets.

### Approach
1. **Filter Negative and Zero Prices**: Since Bob earns money by taking TV sets with negative prices and neither gains nor loses money by taking TV sets with a price of zero, we first filter out all TV sets with prices greater than zero.
2. **Sort the Filtered List**: To maximize earnings, we should prioritize taking the TV sets with the most negative prices first. Therefore, we sort the filtered list of TV sets in ascending order (most negative first).
3. **Select Up to `m` TV Sets**: From the sorted list, we select up to `m` TV sets. If the number of non-positive TV sets is less than `m`, we take all available non-positive TV sets.
4. **Calculate Earnings**: Sum the absolute values of the selected TV sets' prices to determine the maximum earnings.

### Solution Code
```python
n, m = map(int, input().split())
a = list(map(int, input().split()))

# Filter out non-positive values and sort them in ascending order (most negative first)
non_positive = sorted([x for x in a if x <= 0])

# Take up to m elements, the most negative ones
k = min(m, len(non_positive))
total = sum(-x for x in non_positive[:k])

print(total)
```

### Explanation
1. **Input Reading**: The code reads the number of TV sets `n` and the maximum number Bob can carry `m`, followed by the list of TV set prices.
2. **Filtering and Sorting**: We filter out all positive prices and sort the remaining non-positive prices in ascending order. This ensures that the most beneficial (most negative) prices come first.
3. **Selecting TV Sets**: We take the first `k` elements from the sorted list, where `k` is the minimum of `m` and the number of non-positive TV sets. This ensures we take the most beneficial TV sets available.
4. **Summing Earnings**: By summing the absolute values of the selected TV sets' prices, we determine the maximum amount Bob can earn.

This approach efficiently selects the optimal TV sets to maximize earnings using sorting and summation, ensuring both correctness and optimal performance within the problem constraints.
