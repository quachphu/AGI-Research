To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of inversions in a sequence after performing a series of swap operations on an initially sorted sequence of positive integers. An inversion is a pair of indices (i, j) such that i < j and the element at position i is greater than the element at position j.

### Approach
The approach involves three main parts to efficiently compute the inversion count after all swaps:

1. **Case 1: Inversions within Swapped Positions**:
   - Track the positions involved in swaps and their values after each swap.
   - Use a Fenwick Tree (Binary Indexed Tree) to count inversions among these swapped positions.

2. **Case 2: Inversions Between Swapped and Non-Swapped Positions**:
   - For each swapped position, compute how many non-swapped positions (greater than the current position) have values smaller than the current swapped value.

3. **Case 3: Inversions Between Non-Swapped and Swapped Positions**:
   - For each swapped position, compute how many non-swapped positions (smaller than the current position) have values larger than the current swapped value.
