To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of dangerous turns on a bicycle race track. A dangerous turn is one where, if Maria does not turn correctly, she would immediately enter the water. The track is represented as a polygon, and we need to identify such turns based on the geometry of the polygon.

### Approach
1. **Understanding the Track**: The track is a closed polygon starting from the southernmost (and westernmost) point, moving north initially. The track consists of straight sections connected by turns.
2. **Cross Product Calculation**: For each turn (defined by three consecutive points), we calculate the cross product of the vectors formed by the points. The sign of the cross product indicates the direction of the turn (left or right).
3. **Dangerous Turns**: A turn is dangerous if it is a left turn (positive cross product) in the context of the polygon's traversal direction. This indicates a convex vertex where continuing straight would exit the track into the water.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
points = [tuple(map(int, input().split())) for _ in range(n + 1)]

dangerous = 0

# Process turns at points[1] to points[n-1]
for i in range(1, n):
    a = points[i-1]
    b = points[i]
    c = points[i+1]
    dx1 = b[0] - a[0]
    dy1 = b[1] - a[1]
    dx2 = c[0] - b[0]
    dy2 = c[1] - b[1]
    cross = dx1 * dy2 - dy1 * dx2
    if cross > 0:
        dangerous += 1

# Process the turn at points[0] (which is points[n] in the input)
a = points[n-1]
b = points[0]
c = points[1]
dx1 = b[0] - a[0]
dy1 = b[1] - a[1]
dx2 = c[0] - b[0]
dy2 = c[1] - b[1]
cross = dx1 * dy2 - dy1 * dx2
if cross > 0:
    dangerous += 1

print(dangerous)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of the number of straight sections `n` followed by `n+1` points defining the track.
2. **Cross Product Calculation**: For each consecutive triple of points, we compute the cross product of the vectors formed by the points. This helps determine the direction of the turn.
3. **Dangerous Turn Check**: A positive cross product indicates a left turn, which is considered dangerous as it implies a convex vertex where continuing straight would exit the track into the water.
4. **Special Case Handling**: The last turn at the starting point (points[0]) is handled separately to account for the wrap-around in the polygon.

This approach efficiently determines the number of dangerous turns by leveraging geometric properties of the polygon, ensuring an accurate and optimal solution.
