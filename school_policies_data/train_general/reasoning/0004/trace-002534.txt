To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of common tangent lines between two given circles based on their positions and radii. The solution involves analyzing the relative positions of the circles and their radii to classify the scenario into one of the predefined cases.

### Approach
1. **Calculate the Distance Squared Between Centers**: Compute the squared distance between the centers of the two circles to avoid floating-point precision issues.
2. **Sum and Difference of Radii**: Calculate the sum and absolute difference of the radii of the two circles, then square these values for comparison with the squared distance.
3. **Case Analysis**: Compare the squared distance with the squared sum and difference of the radii to determine the number of common tangents:
   - If the squared distance is greater than the squared sum of the radii, the circles are separate, resulting in 4 tangents.
   - If the squared distance equals the squared sum of the radii, the circles are externally tangent, resulting in 3 tangents.
   - If the squared distance is between the squared difference and squared sum of the radii, the circles intersect at two points, resulting in 2 tangents.
   - If the squared distance equals the squared difference of the radii, the circles are internally tangent, resulting in 1 tangent.
   - If the squared distance is less than the squared difference of the radii, one circle is entirely inside the other, resulting in 0 tangents.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    import sys
    input = sys.stdin.read().split()
    c1x, c1y, c1r = map(int, input[0:3])
    c2x, c2y, c2r = map(int, input[3:6])
    dx = c2x - c1x
    dy = c2y - c1y
    d_sq = dx ** 2 + dy ** 2
    sum_r = c1r + c2r
    sum_sq = sum_r ** 2
    diff_r = abs(c1r - c2r)
    diff_sq = diff_r ** 2

    if d_sq > sum_sq:
        print(4)
    elif d_sq == sum_sq:
        print(3)
    elif d_sq > diff_sq:
        print(2)
    elif d_sq == diff_sq:
        print(1)
    else:
        print(0)

main()
```

1. **Reading Input**: The input is read and parsed into the coordinates and radii of the two circles.
2. **Distance Calculation**: The squared distance between the centers is computed using integer arithmetic to avoid precision issues.
3. **Sum and Difference Squared**: The sum and absolute difference of the radii are squared for comparison.
4. **Condition Checks**: The squared distance is compared against the squared sum and difference of the radii to determine the number of common tangents, with each condition leading to the appropriate output based on the geometric relationship of the circles.

This approach efficiently classifies the circles' relationship using basic arithmetic operations and comparisons, ensuring accuracy and optimal performance.
