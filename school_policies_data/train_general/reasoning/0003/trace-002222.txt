To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of squares crossed by a line segment drawn between two points on a grid. The key insight is to use a mathematical formula involving the greatest common divisor (GCD) to efficiently compute the result.

### Approach
1. **Problem Analysis**: The line segment is drawn from the lower-left corner of one square to the lower-left corner of another square. The goal is to count how many squares the segment intersects, excluding the boundaries.
2. **Mathematical Insight**: The number of squares crossed by a line segment between two points can be determined using the formula:
   \[
   \text{number of squares crossed} = dx + dy - \text{gcd}(dx, dy)
   \]
   where \( dx \) is the absolute difference in the x-coordinates and \( dy \) is the absolute difference in the y-coordinates of the two points. The GCD accounts for overlapping crossings where the line crosses both a vertical and horizontal grid line simultaneously.

### Solution Code
```python
import math

A, B, C, D = map(int, input().split())
dx = abs(C - A)
dy = abs(D - B)
g = math.gcd(dx, dy)
print(dx + dy - g)
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of four integers representing the coordinates of the two points.
2. **Calculating Differences**: Compute the absolute differences \( dx \) and \( dy \) between the x-coordinates and y-coordinates of the two points.
3. **GCD Calculation**: Use the GCD of \( dx \) and \( dy \) to account for overlapping crossings.
4. **Applying the Formula**: Subtract the GCD from the sum of \( dx \) and \( dy \) to get the number of squares crossed.

This approach efficiently computes the result using basic arithmetic and the GCD function, ensuring it works even for very large input values due to the constraints.
