To solve this problem, we need to determine the minimum number of minutes Masha needs to spend increasing the lengths of her sticks so that they can form a triangle with positive area. The solution is based on the triangle inequality theorem, which states that the sum of the lengths of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the length of the remaining side.

### Approach
1. **Sort the Sticks**: First, sort the given stick lengths in non-decreasing order. This helps in easily identifying the largest stick, which will be the critical factor in determining the minimum increments needed.
2. **Check Triangle Inequality**: After sorting, let the lengths be \(a, b, c\) where \(a \leq b \leq c\). The key inequality to check is \(a + b > c\). If this inequality holds, no increments are needed. If it does not, we need to calculate the minimum increments required to make the sum of the two smaller sticks greater than the largest stick.
3. **Calculate Required Increments**: The minimum number of minutes required is determined by how much we need to increase the lengths of the smaller sticks so that their sum exceeds the largest stick. This can be computed as \(\max(0, (c + 1) - (a + b))\), ensuring we handle cases where the sum is already sufficient.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    a, b, c = sorted(map(int, input().split()))
    required = max(0, (c + 1) - a - b)
    print(required)

main()
```

### Explanation
- **Sorting the Sticks**: By sorting the stick lengths, we can easily identify the largest stick \(c\) and the two smaller sticks \(a\) and \(b\).
- **Triangle Inequality Check**: The critical check is \(a + b > c\). If this is already true, no increments are needed. If not, we need to determine the difference needed to make this true.
- **Computing the Result**: The formula \(\max(0, (c + 1) - (a + b))\) gives the exact number of increments needed. This ensures that the sum of the two smaller sticks is just enough to exceed the largest stick, which is the minimal requirement for forming a valid triangle.

This approach efficiently determines the minimum increments needed by leveraging sorting and a direct application of the triangle inequality theorem, ensuring optimal performance and correctness.
