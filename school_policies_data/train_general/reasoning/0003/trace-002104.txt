To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of trailing zeros in the decimal representation of the double factorial of a given integer \( N \). The trailing zeros in a number are determined by the number of times 10 is a factor in that number, which in turn is determined by the number of pairs of the prime factors 2 and 5. Since there are usually more factors of 2 than 5, the number of trailing zeros is determined by the number of times 5 is a factor in the number.

### Approach
1. **Understanding Double Factorial**:
   - For even \( N \), the double factorial \( N!! \) is the product of all even numbers from 2 to \( N \).
   - For odd \( N \), the double factorial \( N!! \) is the product of all odd numbers from 1 to \( N \).

2. **Key Insight**:
   - If \( N \) is odd, the product \( N!! \) will consist of only odd numbers, which means there are no factors of 2. Hence, there can be no trailing zeros, and the result is 0.
   - If \( N \) is even, the product \( N!! \) can be expressed as \( 2^{(N/2)} \times (N/2)! \). The number of trailing zeros in \( (N/2)! \) is determined by the number of times 5 is a factor in \( (N/2)! \).

3. **Counting Factors of 5**:
   - For an even \( N \), compute \( k = N/2 \).
   - Calculate the number of times 5 is a factor in \( k! \) by summing the integer divisions of \( k \) by increasing powers of 5 (i.e., \( k//5 + k//25 + k//125 + \ldots \)).

### Solution Code
```python
def count_trailing_zeros():
    import sys
    N = int(sys.stdin.readline())
    if N % 2 != 0:
        print(0)
        return
    k = N // 2
    total = 0
    current = 5
    while current <= k:
        total += k // current
        current *= 5
    print(total)

count_trailing_zeros()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**:
   - The input integer \( N \) is read from standard input.

2. **Check for Odd \( N \)**:
   - If \( N \) is odd, the result is immediately 0 because there are no factors of 2 in the product of odd numbers.

3. **Compute for Even \( N \)**:
   - Convert \( N \) to \( k = N/2 \).
   - Initialize `total` to accumulate the count of factors of 5.
   - Use a loop to iterate through increasing powers of 5 (starting from 5), adding the integer division of \( k \) by each power of 5 to `total`.

This approach efficiently computes the number of trailing zeros by focusing on the factors of 5 in the relevant factorial product, leveraging mathematical properties and efficient looping to handle very large values of \( N \) up to \( 10^{18} \).
