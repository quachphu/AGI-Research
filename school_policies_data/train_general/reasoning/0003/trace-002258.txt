To solve this problem, we need to determine the number of distinct weights of splits for a given integer \( n \). A split of \( n \) is a non-increasing sequence of positive integers that sum to \( n \), and the weight of a split is the number of elements equal to the first element in the sequence.

### Approach
The key insight is to recognize that the possible weights of splits of \( n \) are the integers from 1 up to \( \left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor \) plus one additional weight for the case where all elements are 1. This is because any integer \( k \) from 1 to \( \left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor \) can be the weight of a split by starting with \( k \) elements of 2 followed by 1's. Additionally, the weight \( n \) is always possible when all elements are 1's.

### Solution Code

```python
n = int(input())
print(n // 2 + 1)
```

   - The weight \( n \), achieved by the split consisting of all 1's.
