To solve this problem, we need to compute the probability that a cleaning robot stops at a specified battery room when its battery runs out. The robot moves in a 3x3 grid of rooms, avoiding a junk room, and each movement or attempt to move consumes 1 battery point. The solution involves dynamic programming to model the robot's movements and probabilities.

### Approach
1. **Problem Analysis**: The robot moves in a 3x3 grid, and each movement direction (North, South, East, West) is chosen with equal probability. The robot cannot move into the junk room or outside the grid, and it consumes battery even if it stays in place. The goal is to compute the probability that the robot ends up in the battery room when the battery is exhausted.

2. **Dynamic Programming (DP) Approach**: 
   - **State Definition**: Use a DP table where `dp[k][room]` represents the probability of ending up in the battery room when there are `k` battery points left starting from `room`.
   - **Base Case**: When the battery is 0 (`k=0`), the probability is 1 if the current room is the battery room, otherwise 0.
   - **Transitions**: For each room and each possible direction, compute the next room considering the junk room and grid boundaries. Accumulate the probabilities from all possible next states.

3. **Precompute Transitions**: For each room and direction, precompute the next room considering the junk room to avoid redundant calculations during DP transitions.
