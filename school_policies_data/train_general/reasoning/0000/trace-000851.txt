To solve this problem, we need to find the smallest integer \( m \) such that the binomial coefficient \( \binom{n}{m} \) is even. The solution leverages properties of binary numbers and bitwise operations to efficiently determine the result.

### Approach
1. **Binary Representation Insight**: The binomial coefficient \( \binom{n}{m} \) is even if there is at least one position in the binary representations of \( n \) and \( m \) where \( m \) has a bit set (1) that \( n \) does not. This can be determined using Lucas' theorem.
2. **Power of Two Check**: If \( n \) is of the form \( 2^k - 1 \) (all bits in its binary representation are 1), then \( \binom{n}{m} \) is odd for all \( m \) from 1 to \( n \). The smallest \( m \) in this case is \( n + 1 \), which is the next power of two.
3. **Rightmost Zero Bit**: For other values of \( n \), the smallest \( m \) is determined by the position of the rightmost zero bit in the binary representation of \( n \). This can be found using bitwise operations.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
if (n & (n + 1)) == 0:
    print(n + 1)
else:
    print((n + 1) & (-n - 1))
```

### Explanation
1. **Power of Two Check**: The condition `(n & (n + 1)) == 0` checks if \( n \) is one less than a power of two. If true, \( \binom{n}{m} \) will be odd for all \( m \) from 1 to \( n \), so the answer is \( n + 1 \).
2. **Finding Rightmost Zero**: The expression `(n + 1) & (-n - 1)` computes the position of the rightmost zero in \( n \)'s binary form. This works by leveraging the properties of two's complement arithmetic to isolate the rightmost zero bit and convert it to a power of two, giving the smallest \( m \).

This approach efficiently handles the constraints using bitwise operations, ensuring optimal performance even for very large values of \( n \) up to \( 10^{18} \).
