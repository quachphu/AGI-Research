To solve this problem, we need to determine if any part of a white sheet of paper remains visible after placing two black sheets on top of it. The solution involves checking if the union of the two black sheets completely covers the white sheet. If there is any part of the white sheet not covered by the black sheets, the answer is "YES"; otherwise, it is "NO".

### Approach
1. **Calculate the Area of the White Sheet**: Compute the area of the white sheet using its coordinates.
2. **Check Overlap with Each Black Sheet**: Determine the area of the white sheet that overlaps with each black sheet.
3. **Check Overlap of Black Sheets Intersection**: Determine the area of the white sheet that is covered by the intersection of the two black sheets. This helps in avoiding double-counting the overlapping area covered by both black sheets.
4. **Compute Total Covered Area**: Use the inclusion-exclusion principle to calculate the total area of the white sheet covered by the two black sheets.
5. **Compare Covered Area with White Sheet Area**: If the total covered area equals the area of the white sheet, the answer is "NO"; otherwise, it is "YES".

### Solution Code
```python
x1, y1, x2, y2 = map(int, input().split())
x3, y3, x4, y4 = map(int, input().split())
x5, y5, x6, y6 = map(int, input().split())

white = (x1, y1, x2, y2)
black1 = (x3, y3, x4, y4)
black2 = (x5, y5, x6, y6)

def overlap_area(a, b):
    a_x1, a_y1, a_x2, a_y2 = a
    b_x1, b_y1, b_x2, b_y2 = b
    x_start = max(a_x1, b_x1)
    x_end = min(a_x2, b_x2)
    y_start = max(a_y1, b_y1)
    y_end = min(a_y2, b_y2)
    if x_start >= x_end or y_start >= y_end:
        return 0
    return (x_end - x_start) * (y_end - y_start)

a1 = overlap_area(white, black1)
a2 = overlap_area(white, black2)

# Compute intersection of black1 and black2

covered = a1 + a2 - a_intersect
```

3. **Intersection of Black Sheets**: The intersection area of the two black sheets is computed to avoid double-counting the overlapping region.
4. **Inclusion-Exclusion Principle**: The total covered area on the white sheet is calculated by adding the areas covered by each black sheet and subtracting the area covered by their intersection.
5. **Comparison**: The total covered area is compared with the area of the white sheet to determine if any part remains visible.

This approach efficiently checks the visibility of the white sheet by leveraging geometric calculations and the inclusion-exclusion principle, ensuring an optimal and accurate solution.
