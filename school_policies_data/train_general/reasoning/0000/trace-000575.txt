To solve this problem, we need to arrange children in a circle such that the maximum difference in height between any two adjacent children is minimized. The approach involves sorting the heights and then strategically placing the elements to minimize these differences.

### Approach
1. **Sort the Heights**: Start by sorting the list of children's heights in non-decreasing order.
2. **Split into Even and Odd Indices**: After sorting, split the list into two groups:
   - Elements at even indices (0-based) in their original order.
   - Elements at odd indices in reverse order.
3. **Combine Groups**: Concatenate the even-indexed elements with the reversed odd-indexed elements. This arrangement helps in minimizing the maximum adjacent difference by creating a "mountain" shape where heights increase to a peak and then decrease.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
heights = list(map(int, input().split()))
heights.sort()

even = [heights[i] for i in range(0, n, 2)]
odd = [heights[i] for i in range(1, n, 2)]

result = even + odd[::-1]
print(' '.join(map(str, result)))
```

### Explanation
1. **Sorting**: By sorting the heights, we ensure that the smallest and largest values are at the extremes of the sorted list.
2. **Splitting Indices**: Dividing the sorted list into even and odd indices allows us to create two groups. The even-indexed elements are taken in their natural order, while the odd-indexed elements are taken in reverse order. This helps in distributing the larger values closer to each other, reducing the maximum difference between adjacent elements.
3. **Combining Groups**: The combination of even-indexed elements followed by reversed odd-indexed elements forms a structure where the heights rise to a peak and then fall, minimizing the maximum difference between consecutive elements in the circle.

This approach efficiently minimizes the maximum adjacent difference by leveraging sorted order and strategic placement, ensuring an optimal arrangement in O(n log n) time complexity due to sorting.
