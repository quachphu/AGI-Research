To solve this problem, we need to determine the round in which two specific teams, identified by their IDs, will meet in a playoff-style tournament. The teams are sorted by their IDs in each round and paired consecutively. The goal is to find the earliest round where the two teams meet, or if they meet in the final round.

### Approach
1. **Problem Analysis**: The teams are paired in each round based on their sorted IDs. The key insight is that two teams will meet in the earliest round where they are consecutive in the sorted list of remaining teams. This can be determined by examining the binary representation of their positions relative to each other.
2. **Key Insight**: The earliest round where the two teams meet can be found by determining the highest bit where their adjusted positions (IDs minus 1) differ. This bit position helps us compute the round number.
3. **Algorithm**:
   - Calculate the adjusted positions of the two teams (subtracting 1 from each ID).
   - Use XOR to find the differing bit positions.
   - Determine the most significant bit (MSB) position of the XOR result to find the earliest round.
   - Compare this round with the total number of rounds in the tournament to check if it's the final round.

### Solution Code
```python
def main():
    import sys
    n, a, b = map(int, sys.stdin.readline().split())
    x = min(a, b)
    y = max(a, b)
    x_adj = x - 1
    y_adj = y - 1
    d = x_adj ^ y_adj
    msb = d.bit_length() - 1
    k = msb + 1
    
    total_rounds = 0
    temp = n
    while temp > 1:
        temp //= 2
        total_rounds += 1
    
    print("Final!" if k == total_rounds else k)

main()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input consists of three integers: the number of teams `n`, and the IDs of the two teams `a` and `b`.
2. **Adjusting IDs**: Convert the team IDs to zero-based indices by subtracting 1 from each.
3. **XOR Operation**: Compute the XOR of the adjusted IDs to find the differing bit positions.
4. **Most Significant Bit (MSB)**: Determine the MSB of the XOR result to identify the earliest round where the teams meet.
5. **Total Rounds Calculation**: Calculate the total number of rounds by repeatedly halving the number of teams until it reaches 1.
6. **Result Determination**: Compare the computed round with the total rounds to decide if it's the final round or not, and print the result accordingly.

This approach efficiently determines the meeting round using bitwise operations, ensuring optimal performance even for the upper limit of input constraints.
