### Approach
The key insight is that \( G(n) \) corresponds to the \( n \)-th Fibonacci number. This is derived from the observation that the number of valid compositions (ordered sums) of \( n \) using only odd integers follows the Fibonacci recurrence relation. For example, \( G(4) = 3 \) which is the 4th Fibonacci number.

To compute Fibonacci numbers efficiently for very large \( n \) (up to \( 10^{18} \)), we use the fast doubling method. This method allows us to compute Fibonacci numbers in \( O(\log n) \) time using matrix exponentiation principles, which is crucial given the problem constraints.
