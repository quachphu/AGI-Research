To solve this problem, we need to find the greatest common divisor (GCD) of two natural numbers using the Euclidean algorithm. The Euclidean algorithm efficiently computes the GCD by repeatedly applying the division algorithm, reducing the problem size each time until the remainder becomes zero.

### Approach
The Euclidean algorithm is based on the principle that the GCD of two numbers also divides their difference. For any two integers \( x \) and \( y \) where \( x \geq y \), the GCD of \( x \) and \( y \) is the same as the GCD of \( y \) and \( x \% y \). This process is repeated until \( y \) becomes zero, at which point \( x \) is the GCD.

1. **Read Input**: Read the two integers from the standard input.
2. **Apply Euclidean Algorithm**: Continuously replace the larger number by the remainder of the division of the larger number by the smaller number until the smaller number becomes zero.
3. **Output Result**: The non-zero number remaining after the process is the GCD.

### Solution Code
```python
def compute_gcd():
    a, b = map(int, input().split())
    while b != 0:
        a, b = b, a % b
    print(a)

compute_gcd()
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The function reads two integers from the standard input, which are split and converted into integers.
2. **Euclidean Algorithm**: The while loop continues until the second number \( b \) becomes zero. In each iteration, \( a \) is replaced by \( b \), and \( b \) is replaced by \( a \% b \). This reduces the problem size each time by focusing on the remainder of the division.
3. **Termination and Result**: When \( b \) becomes zero, the loop exits, and the current value of \( a \) is the GCD, which is then printed.

This approach ensures that we efficiently compute the GCD using the properties of the Euclidean algorithm, which has a time complexity of \( O(\log(\min(a, b))) \), making it very efficient even for large values of \( a \) and \( b \).
