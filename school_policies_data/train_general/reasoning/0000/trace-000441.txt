To solve this problem, Kolya needs to maximize the liters of kefir he can buy with his rubles. He can choose between plastic bottles and glass bottles, where glass bottles can be returned for a partial refund. The goal is to determine the optimal strategy considering the costs and refunds.

### Approach
1. **Effective Cost Comparison**: Compare the effective cost of a glass bottle (cost after refund) with the cost of a plastic bottle.
2. **Plastic Bottles Only**: If the effective cost of a glass bottle is greater than or equal to the cost of a plastic bottle, Kolya should buy only plastic bottles.
3. **Glass Bottles Strategy**: If the effective cost of a glass bottle is cheaper, calculate the maximum number of glass bottles Kolya can buy, considering the initial cost and subsequent effective costs. Then use any remaining money to buy plastic bottles.

### Solution Code
```python
n = int(input())
a = int(input())
b = int(input())
c = int(input())

effective_glass = b - c

if effective_glass >= a:
    print(n // a)
else:
    if n < b:
        print(n // a)
    else:
        k = (n - b) // effective_glass + 1
        remaining = n - k * effective_glass
        total = k + remaining // a
        print(total)
```

### Explanation
1. **Input Reading**: Read the initial amount of rubles and the costs of the bottles.
2. **Effective Cost Calculation**: Compute the effective cost of a glass bottle after considering the refund.
3. **Decision Making**:
   - If the effective cost of a glass bottle is not cheaper than a plastic bottle, Kolya buys only plastic bottles.
   - If Kolya cannot afford even one glass bottle, he buys as many plastic bottles as possible.
   - Otherwise, calculate the maximum number of glass bottles Kolya can buy and use the remaining money for plastic bottles.

This approach ensures that Kolya maximizes the liters of kefir by optimally using his rubles, considering both immediate costs and potential refunds.
