To solve this problem, we need to determine the smallest positive integer \( x \) that is divisible by \( d \) and does not lie within the segment \([l, r]\) for each query. 

### Approach
1. **Check if \( d \) is outside the segment**: If \( d \) is less than \( l \) or greater than \( r \), then \( d \) itself is the answer since it is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of \( d \) and outside the segment.
2. **Find the largest multiple of \( d \) less than \( l \)**: If \( d \) is within the segment \([l, r]\), compute the largest multiple of \( d \) that is less than \( l \). This is done by integer division of \( l-1 \) by \( d \) and then multiplying back by \( d \).
3. **Find the smallest multiple of \( d \) greater than \( r \)**: Similarly, compute the smallest multiple of \( d \) that is greater than \( r \) using integer division of \( r \) by \( d \), incrementing the quotient by 1, and then multiplying back by \( d \).
4. **Determine the minimum valid multiple**: The valid answer will be the minimum between the largest multiple less than \( l \) (if it exists) and the smallest multiple greater than \( r \).

### Solution Code
```python
q = int(input())
for _ in range(q):
    l, r, d = map(int, input().split())
    if d < l or d > r:
        print(d)
    else:
        candidate1 = (l - 1) // d * d
        possible = []
        if candidate1 >= 1:
            possible.append(candidate1)
        candidate2 = (r // d + 1) * d
        possible.append(candidate2)
        print(min(possible))
```

### Explanation
- **Check for \( d \) outside the segment**: If \( d \) is outside the segment \([l, r]\), it is immediately the answer.
- **Compute candidate multiples**: If \( d \) is within the segment, compute the largest valid multiple of \( d \) less than \( l \) and the smallest valid multiple of \( d \) greater than \( r \).
- **Select the minimum valid multiple**: Compare the valid candidates (if they exist) and select the smallest one to ensure the result is the minimal positive integer meeting the criteria.

This approach efficiently handles each query in constant time \( O(1) \), making it suitable for the given constraints.
