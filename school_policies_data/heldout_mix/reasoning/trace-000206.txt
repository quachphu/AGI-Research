To solve this problem, we need to determine the total length of the trajectory of a ray of Mysterious Light fired from a point inside an equilateral triangle formed by mirrors. The ray reflects off the mirrors and its own trajectory until it returns to the starting point. The solution involves leveraging the greatest common divisor (GCD) to simplify the problem.

### Approach
1. **Understanding Reflections**: The problem can be modeled using reflections in a grid of mirrored triangles. The key insight is that the ray's path can be considered as a straight line through these mirrored triangles.
2. **GCD Insight**: The total length of the trajectory is determined by the GCD of the side length of the triangle (N) and the distance from the vertex (X). The GCD helps in breaking down the problem into smaller, manageable segments.
3. **Formula Derivation**: The total length of the trajectory is given by the formula \(3 \times (N - \text{GCD}(N, X))\). This formula is derived from the observation that the ray's path forms segments whose lengths are multiples of the GCD, leading to a simplified calculation.

### Solution Code
```python
import math

n, x = map(int, input().split())
g = math.gcd(n, x)
print(3 * (n - g))
```

### Explanation
1. **Reading Input**: The input values N (side length of the triangle) and X (distance from vertex A to the rifle's position) are read.
2. **GCD Calculation**: The GCD of N and X is computed to determine the fundamental segment length that repeats in the ray's path.
3. **Trajectory Length Calculation**: Using the formula \(3 \times (N - \text{GCD}(N, X))\), the total length of the ray's trajectory is calculated. This formula efficiently combines the geometric properties of the problem with number theory to provide the solution.

This approach efficiently handles the constraints and provides the correct result by leveraging mathematical insights into the problem structure.
